Every few weeks another acronym enters the search conversation, and right now the three fighting for attention are **SEO**, **GEO**, and **AEO**. They are related, they overlap, and they are not the same thing. If you treat them as interchangeable, you end up doing three jobs badly instead of one job that compounds. Here is how I define each and how I sequence them.

## SEO: earning the ranking

Search Engine Optimization is still the foundation. It is the practice of making a page understandable, crawlable, and authoritative enough that a search engine ranks it for a query. Technical health, site architecture, internal linking, and content that matches intent — none of that goes away. Everything downstream depends on it.

If your pages cannot be crawled and rendered, no amount of AI-era tactics will save you. SEO is the price of entry, not the whole game.

## AEO: winning the answer

Answer Engine Optimization is about being the source a system quotes when it answers a question directly — think featured snippets, People Also Ask, voice results, and Google's AI Overviews. AEO rewards content that is structured, unambiguous, and easy to extract: clear definitions, direct answers near the top, well-formed headings, and schema that removes guesswork.

> AEO asks a sharper question than SEO: not "can you rank for this query?" but "can a machine lift a clean, correct answer straight off your page?"

## GEO: being cited by the model

Generative Engine Optimization is the newest layer. It targets large language models — ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity — and the goal is to be _cited_ inside a generated response, not just ranked in a list of links. GEO leans on entity clarity, consistent brand signals across the web, retrieval-ready formatting, and genuine topical authority the model has seen enough times to trust.

The mechanics differ from classic SEO because the surface is different. There is often no ranked list — there is one synthesized answer, and either your brand is in it or it is not.

## How they fit together

These are layers, not rivals. The way I sequence them:

- **Start with SEO.** Fix crawlability, architecture, and intent-matched content. This is the base everything else stands on.
- **Layer AEO on top.** Restructure your best pages so answers are extractable — definitions up top, clean headings, complete schema.
- **Extend into GEO.** Build entity consistency, strengthen topical authority, and format for retrieval so models cite you with confidence.

Done in that order, each layer reinforces the last. Skip the base and you are optimizing for citations on a site that engines cannot reliably read in the first place.
